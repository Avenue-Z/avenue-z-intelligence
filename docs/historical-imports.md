# Historical review imports

The shared Claude reports supplied by the user are reference evidence. The app accepts dated text reports, archives of up to 31 reports, and normalized historical observations. Imports are stored in the shared database and retain original store dates; performance values are not embedded in the application source.

The October 8 migration reads 11 Beam reviews (September 27–October 7) and 16 Kind Patches reviews (September 19–October 8, with gaps). Beam account-level Meta spend uses the report's corrected figures. The known understated creative breakdown is excluded from normalized observations and retained, with its correction, in the original review text. Kind Patches headline observations use the explicitly closed October 7 report, including its October 6 and September 30 comparators; morning partial snapshots are retained in the review archive and are not recast as closed days.

Reported rounded CVR and ATC remain `reported_conversion_rate` and `reported_atc_rate`. No converting-session or checkout counts are invented from rounded percentages. Rates are not added across dates or segments. Full-day counts, revenue and spend support historical charts; missing dates stay gaps. Historical quality does not qualify live coverage or a live efficiency verdict.

Imported action items carry a dated source-review link and the original measurement caveats. They begin open and unassigned. Owners and statuses are team records. Qualified automatic signals require paired sessions and converting-session coverage for every selected and benchmark day, including continuous intervals through the matched intraday cutoff. The Action queue is the shared tracking surface; tracking the same signal twice does not create duplicate tasks. Closing suppresses that signal for its current period and scope.

Before enabling live ingestion, reconcile source definitions and client scopes against authorized provider or warehouse totals. No source credentials or recurring ingestion schedule are active in this migration.
